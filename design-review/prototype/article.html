Perplexity launches its own take on AI agents for work

Always-on “Teammates” with a role, a shared workspace, and the memory of everything you already did in Perplexity. The product is not the model — it is the memory.

Nasser · Editor, Dhofar University · 6 November 2026 · 4 min read

In 30 seconds

What happened
Perplexity announced Teammates: always-on agents that take a role inside a workspace and reuse projects, skills, and memory from its existing tools.
Why it matters
The competitive ground moved from answer quality to persistence: who holds the agent's context, and who can inspect what it did.
In class
COMP 340: treat the agent as a service with state. Define its contract, its permissions, and its audit log before arguing about the model.

Perplexity has announced a set of work-focused agents it calls Teammates. Unlike a chat session that forgets you when the tab closes, a Teammate is meant to keep a role inside a workspace: it can pick up an existing project, reuse the skills you have already set up, and draw on the memory stored in Perplexity's tools. The company says the agents integrate with Perplexity Computer, its system for running agents on a device.

That sounds like a feature list. It is really a change in what the product is. For two years, the assistant was a box you typed into. Here, the assistant is a participant with a name, a scope, and a history. The model still matters, but the interesting engineering sits around it: what the agent is allowed to do, what it remembers, and what a human can replay afterwards.

Memory is the product

Carrying context across sessions is what makes an agent feel like a colleague rather than a tool. It is also what makes a small mistake expensive. If the agent mislabels one project, that label can quietly travel into later summaries, tickets, and code reviews. The risk is not that the model is wrong once — it is that the error becomes persistent and looks like institutional knowledge.

Memory is what turns a chatbot into a colleague — and what turns one bad assumption into a systemic one.

The practical questions are therefore the unglamorous ones. What can the agent change without asking? Which actions require a human signature? Where is the log, and can a reviewer see the exact inputs that produced an action six weeks ago? Teams that answer these before deployment will get the productivity; teams that skip them will get an incident report.

Term used in this story Agentic system — software in which a model plans steps, calls tools, and acts toward a goal instead of producing a single answer.

What to take to class

In a software engineering course, this is a clean exercise in boundaries. Ask students to write the service contract for a “teammate”: its inputs, its state, its permissions, and the events it must emit. Then ask what happens when two agents claim the same task, or when the memory contradicts the latest instruction. Those are design questions with exam-grade answers, and they are exactly what the product teams are still figuring out.

The bottom line: the announcement is less about a better model than about a new boundary — between what an agent may do alone and what needs a person. Read it that way and it becomes useful beyond Perplexity.

Check yourself — 3 questions

Same questions the editor would ask in a tutorial. Answers reveal instantly.
